## Context

The catalog carries one multi-author work: `castelnuovo-enriques-1897-surfaces-algebriques`. Its card
shows Castelnuovo's portrait and not Enriques', because `catalog-card-portraits` (archived
2026-09-14) listed "a second portrait for multi-author works" under Out of scope, and
`catalog-card-portraits-phone` repeated the exclusion.

That exclusion's stated reason is narrower than it looks: "splitting the strip into two half-height
images would break the uniform 69px column for a single card". Splitting the 69px column
**side-by-side** would indeed leave two 34px slivers. Splitting it **top-to-bottom** leaves the
column at 69px and divides the other axis, which the reason does not address.

Measured on the built site at 1280px, before any change:

| | |
|---|---|
| Castelnuovo–Enriques card, desktop | **141px** tall, portrait box 69 × 141 |
| Same card, phone layout (390px viewport) | portrait box **69 × 181** |
| Catalog card heights, all 20 works | 108–219px |
| Committed derivative | 138 × 172, aspect **0.802**, `TOP_BIAS` 0.18, head-and-shoulders |

Both `portrait-thumb.jpg` files are committed and both already carry a `thumb_url` in `works.json`,
so the join the catalog already performs for the lead author supplies the second one unchanged.

Everything below was prototyped against the running site — the real card in the real list, then a
parameterised viewer — rather than reasoned about on paper. Where a number appears it was measured.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**

- Both authors' faces on the one card that has two, without changing the 69px column, the card's
  height, either layout, the breakpoint, or any other card.
- A division that reads as a deliberate cut rather than as two stacked stamps or a rendering seam.
- Each face pointing at the person in it, preserving the meaning rule `catalog-portrait-links-author`
  established.
- No new image assets, no pipeline step, no CI change, no new build-time data.

**Non-Goals:**

- Three or more authors. No such work exists; a 69px box cannot carry three legible faces, and the
  answer is better designed against a real work than invented now.
- A pair in which one author has no portrait. Falls back to today's single-portrait behaviour.
- Any change to `/authors/`, `/authors/<slug>/`, journal pages, the timeline, search, or the work
  page.
- A second derivative size, a tighter face crop, or any change to `make_portrait_thumb.py`.
- Exposing the portrait links to assistive technology. That question is orthogonal and unchanged.

## Decisions

### 1. Stack the split; do not divide the column

The cut runs across the box, not down it. Each face then occupies roughly 69 × H/2 rather than
34 × H.

A side-by-side split at 69px leaves each face 34px wide. Cover-cropping the 138 × 172 derivative into
34 × 141 shows 26% of its width — a nose and one eye. Widening the strip to make that work would
break the uniform column the archived exclusion was protecting, and the exclusion would then be
right. Stacking keeps the column at 69px, which is what made this change worth reconsidering at all.

### 2. Two clipped layers, not a composited image

The box holds two `<img>`, each inside its own wedge element carrying a `clip-path: polygon(...)`.

Alternatives considered:

- *A pre-composited derivative* — one JPEG with both faces and the cut already baked in, generated by
  `make_portrait_thumb.py`. Rejected on three counts: it puts a work-level asset into an
  author-keyed store; it needs Pillow to produce a file the site could assemble for free; and it
  collapses the two faces into one image, which makes per-wedge links impossible.
- *A `mask-image` linear-gradient with a hard colour stop* — equivalent geometry and slightly shorter
  to write, but a gradient cannot carry two independently positioned images, and the seam would have
  to be faked with a third stop rather than being the box showing through.

### 3. The slant is specified in px, never as a percentage

The cut is `calc(50% ± 4px)` at each edge: a fixed 8px drop across the box's 69px width.

A percentage drop distorts with the box. Catalog cards run 108–219px, so a drop of "10% of the box"
renders at 9° on the shortest card and 18° on the tallest — the slant would visibly differ down a
list whose every other geometry is constant. The px form renders 6.6° everywhere, and this is the
one property of the cut that must not vary.

### 4. 8px, 6.6° — gentle, and gentle for a reason

The angle is small enough to read as a tilt rather than as a graphic device. It is also the safer
choice for the faces, which is not obvious and was initially got backwards.

Because the cut pivots about the box's midpoint, the slant does not change how much of either face
is visible: the upper wedge's area is the box's width times the cut's height, 69 × cy, at every
angle. What the angle changes is the *shape* of what is hidden. A shallow cut hides a thin even band;
a steep cut hides a triangle of the same total area concentrated in one corner.

At the lower wedge that corner is where the second author's crown sits. Prototyped at 19.2°, the cut
dips to 82.5px on the left edge while Enriques' crown sits around 63–70px, so his forehead falls
inside the hidden triangle and is sheared. At 6.6° the cut only dips to 73.5px and the problem
largely disappears. The gentle slant needs less compensation to be correct, not more.

An intermediate measurement — the fraction of the derivative surviving inside each *image box*,
before the wedge clips it — favours the steep cut (98% against 89% at 141px). That quantity never
reaches the reader and is not the one to design against; it is recorded here only because it is easy
to reach for and misleading.

### 5. A 2px seam, coloured by the box, not by a literal

The wedges are offset from each other by 2px so the box's own background shows through. That
background is the card's `--border` token, so the seam re-colours itself in dark mode with no
second rule and no theme-specific literal.

Without a seam the two photographs meet directly and the cut reads as a rendering artefact — two
images that failed to line up — rather than as a decision. Measured at 0px in the prototype, that is
exactly how it looks.

### 6. One link per wedge

Each wedge is its own `<a>` to its own author: upper-left to the first, lower-right to the second.

This revisits, rather than contradicts, `catalog-portrait-links-author` decision 1, which rejected
"a split target". That rejection was made about a card showing **one** face, where splitting the hit
area between two destinations is arbitrary and invisible. With two faces the split is the only
honest mapping: each half of the box depicts a different person, and a reader who clicks a face
expects that person.

`clip-path` clips pointer events as well as paint, so the hit regions follow the diagonal exactly.
Verified with `elementFromPoint` at six positions on a prototype: the top-left and top-right corners
and the point below the cut's right-hand end all resolve to Castelnuovo; the bottom corners and the
point above the cut's left-hand end resolve to Enriques; the seam itself falls through to the box.
This was measured rather than assumed, because the whole decision rests on it.

The accessibility rationale survives unchanged and is now doubly satisfied: **both** authors are
already links in the card's meta row, so both wedges remain redundant paths to already-announced,
already-focusable destinations. A paired card adds two hidden links and no new destination.

### 7. No fat-corner nudge

An earlier prototype slid each face horizontally toward the corner where its wedge is tallest, to
keep the lower crown clear of a steep cut. At 6.6° it is dropped.

Two measurements retired it. First, the defect it fixed is a steep-cut defect (decision 4); at this
angle there is little left to correct. Second, at 141px the wedge's image box is *wider* than the
derivative, so the image overflows vertically and not horizontally — which means the horizontal
component of `object-position` has nothing to slide, and renders identically at 0% and 16%. It would
do something only on boxes taller than ~172px, which is to say the phone layout and the tallest
cards, and there it is not needed.

Dropping it removes two custom properties and a paragraph of explanation from the CSS.

### 8. Each image box reaches the cut's lowest point

A wedge's `<img>` is sized to `calc(50% + 4px + 2px)` — down to the cut's lowest edge plus a 2px
overlap — and anchored to its own end of the box, top for the upper wedge and bottom for the lower.
The overlap keeps the seam free of a sub-pixel gap at fractional card heights.

This couples the image box's height to the slant, with one consequence worth recording. A wedge is
the first portrait box on this surface that can be **wider** than the derivative's 0.802 aspect,
which makes the crop run vertically where a single portrait's never does. It does so when
`0.5H + 6 < 86`, i.e. on boxes shorter than 160px:

| Box height | Wedge image box | Aspect | Crop | Visible |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 108px (shortest catalog card) | 69 × 60 | 1.15 | vertical | 70% of height |
| **141px (this card, desktop)** | 69 × 76.5 | 0.90 | vertical | **89% of height** |
| 181px (this card, phone) | 69 × 96.5 | 0.72 | horizontal | 89% of width |
| 219px (tallest catalog card) | 69 × 115.5 | 0.60 | horizontal | 75% of width |

The vertical framing is therefore anchored near the top of the derivative, so the crop spends itself
on the collar and coat below the face. Both derivatives are head-and-shoulders with `TOP_BIAS` 0.18;
at the worst case in the table the retained 70% still clears both chins. The spec states the
invariant as a property of the framing rather than of the box's aspect for exactly this reason.

Note that the phone layout, which was the constrained case for a single portrait, is the *best* case
for a pair: at 69 × 96.5 the wedge is within a hair of the derivative's own aspect.

### 9. Exactly two authors, both with portraits

The paired branch is taken only when `authors.length === 2` and both resolve to a `thumb_url`. Every
other shape — three or more authors, a pair with one portrait missing, no author at all — falls
through to the existing code path untouched.

Alternatives considered:

- *First two of N.* Silently hides a third author behind a rule the reader cannot see, and makes the
  card claim a two-author work. Worse than showing one face honestly.
- *A monogram wedge for the missing portrait.* A single letter centred in a triangle, at roughly
  34px of usable height. Prototyping was not needed to reject it.

Neither excluded case exists in the corpus today — it maxes out at two authors and both have
portraits — so both branches are guards rather than visible behaviour, and are specified so that the
first work to hit one gets a defined answer rather than a surprise.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- **Each face is roughly half the size it would be alone.** → Accepted; this is the trade the change
  exists to make. Reviewed at all four heights in the table, both men stay recognisable, including
  on the 108px card no two-author work currently produces.
- **The crop turns vertical below 160px, which no portrait box on this site has done before.** →
  Mitigated by top-anchored framing (decision 8) and stated in the spec as an invariant on the
  framing, so a later change cannot satisfy the letter by widening the box.
- **Two more clip paths and a second image on one card.** → One extra 3.5KB lazy-loaded request, on
  one card. `clip-path` on a static polygon is composited and never animates.
- **The paired branch is exercised by exactly one work.** → The two fallback branches have no live
  coverage at all and are guards by construction (decision 9); the shipped branch is verified against
  the real card at both layouts.
- **A future work with three authors will look inconsistent** beside a paired card. → Accepted
  deliberately and scoped out; the rule is stated in the spec so the inconsistency is a known
  position rather than an oversight.

## Open Questions

- The image box's height is tied to the slant (decision 8), which is what makes the gentle cut crop
  vertically at 141px. Pinning it to a fixed generous value instead would decouple the two and buy
  back most of that crop while keeping 6.6°. It moves where each face sits vertically, so it wants a
  look rather than a calculation, and it is not needed for the invariant to hold. Deferred; revisit
  only if the framing proves fussy in review.
